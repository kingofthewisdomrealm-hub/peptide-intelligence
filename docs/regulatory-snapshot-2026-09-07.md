# Regulatory snapshot — 7 September 2026

Treat peptide legality as **three different legal worlds**.

## World 1 — FDA-approved finished drugs

Insulin, semaglutide, tirzepatide, liraglutide, tesamorelin (Egrifta), bremelanotide (Vyleesi), afamelanotide (Scenesse), somatropin products, elamipretide (Forzinity, accelerated approval for Barth syndrome, 2025), and others.

**FACT:** Approval is for a **named product + indication**, not for a raw powder with the same amino-acid sequence.

## World 2 — Compounding (503A / 503B)

A substance may be compounded only if it has a USP/NF monograph, is a component of an approved drug, is on the 503A/503B bulks list, or (for some 503B cases) is on FDA’s shortage list.

- Semaglutide and tirzepatide shortage compounding has ended.
- FDA has **proposed not** to add semaglutide, tirzepatide, or liraglutide to the 503B bulks list (clinical-need standard not met). That proposal is not the same as “they were removed from a list they were never on.”
- **July 23–24, 2026 PCAC** recommended adding six peptide families to the **503A bulks list**: BPC-157, KPV, TB-500, MOTS-c, Semax, Epitalon. It did **not** recommend Emideltide/DSIP.
- FDA scientists argued the opposite: poorly characterized chemistry, thin human data, immunogenicity risk. The votes were close.
- The vote is **advisory**. FDA must still do rulemaking.
- **As of 7 September 2026 those six are still not lawful 503A bulk substances.** A recommendation is not compounding authorization and is not drug approval.

## World 3 — Research-chemical / grey / underground

Vials labeled “not for human use,” imported powders, med-spa injectables with no batch identity you can trust. Contamination, mislabeling, and sterility failures live here. Map when asked. Do not treat as medicine.

## WADA 2026 (in force 1 Jan 2026)

Always-on bans that matter here:

- **S0:** unapproved substances, including **BPC-157**.
- **S2.2.3:** GH, analogues, fragments (AOD-9604, hGH 176–191).
- **S2.2.4:** GHRH analogues (CJC-1295, sermorelin, tesamorelin), GHS/GHRPs (ipamorelin, GHRP-2/6, hexarelin), and **ibutamoren / MK-677** (not a peptide).
- **S2.3:** thymosin-β4 and derivatives, **explicitly TB-500**.
- Kisspeptin analogues also sit in S2.2.1.

Competitive athletes: assume banned until your anti-doping body says otherwise in writing.
